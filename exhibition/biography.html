STAGE I · EARLY LIFE

Kanpur Beginnings (1923 – 1941)

Harish-Chandra was born on October 11, 1923, in Kanpur. His father, Chandrakishore, served as an executive engineer with the Public Works Department. Early life at home was immersed in classical literature, painting, music, and an intense discipline that characterized his entire scholarly career.

STAGE II · EDUCATION & MENTORSHIP

Allahabad & Cambridge (1941 – 1947)

After completing his M.Sc. in Physics at the University of Allahabad under Prof. K. S. Krishnan, he joined the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore under Homi Bhabha. In 1945, he proceeded to Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, completing his Ph.D. under Nobel Laureate Paul A. M. Dirac on relativistic wave equations.

STAGE III · ACADEMIC CAREER & CONVERSION

Princeton & Columbia (1947 – 1963)

Visiting the Institute for Advanced Study as Dirac’s assistant, Harish-Chandra engaged with Hermann Weyl and Claude Chevalley. Realizing the inherent mathematical ambiguity in quantum electrodynamics of that era, he resolutely moved to pure mathematics, joining Columbia University in 1950.

STAGE IV · MONUMENTAL CONTRIBUTIONS

The Harmonic Analysis Cathedral (1963 – 1983)

Appointed IBM von Neumann Professor at the Institute for Advanced Study in 1963 and elected Fellow of the Royal Society in 1973. He single-handedly developed the representation theory of reductive Lie groups, the Plancherel formula, and discrete series, creating the foundational pillars for the Langlands Program.
