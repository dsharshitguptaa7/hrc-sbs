BIOGRAPHICAL MONOGRAPH
A TITAN OF MATHEMATICAL REASON

Harish-Chandra, FRS

11 October 1923 (Kanpur) — 16 October 1983 (Princeton)

Born in Kanpur, Harish-Chandra transcended disciplines from theoretical physics under P. A. M. Dirac to almost single-handedly constructing the monumental edifice of infinite-dimensional representation theory and non-commutative harmonic analysis on reductive Lie groups.

HARISH-CHANDRA, FRS
Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton (IBM von Neumann Professor of Mathematics).
“He was a man of intense concentration, profound aesthetic sensibility, and immense mathematical stamina. He worked with the dedication of an ascetic and the vision of a poet.”
Prof. Robert Langlands
Professor Emeritus, Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton
CHRONOLOGICAL PATHWAY
FROM KANPUR TO PRINCETON

The Journey of Harish-Chandra

A precision chronology tracking his formative years, education, mentors, mathematical transformation, and timeless contributions.

1923

Early Life & Childhood in Kanpur

Born on October 11, 1923, in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh (then United Provinces). His father, Chandrakishore, was an executive engineer with the Public Works Department. Harish-Chandra spent his early childhood studying at home under tutors and developed an early affinity for literature, painting, and music.

Kanpur Roots
1941 – 1943

University of Allahabad & Prof. K. S. Krishnan

Completed his M.Sc. in Physics at the University of Allahabad in 1943, securing first rank. He studied under the celebrated physicist Prof. K. S. Krishnan (FRS), who recognized his extraordinary analytical gifts and recommended him to Homi J. Bhabha at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore.

Allahabad & IISc Bangalore
1945 – 1947

Cambridge University & Ph.D. under P. A. M. Dirac

Went to the University of Cambridge as a research student under Paul A. M. Dirac. In 1947, he was awarded his Ph.D. for his thesis on relativistic wave equations. During this era, he accompanied Dirac to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton as his scientific assistant.

Cambridge University · Ph.D.
1947 – 1950

The Great Pivot: From Theoretical Physics to Pure Mathematics

At Princeton and Columbia, interactions with Hermann Weyl, Claude Chevalley, and Emil Artin convinced Harish-Chandra that physics was overly imprecise for his standards of rigour. He decided to turn his full intellect toward the representation theory of semisimple Lie groups.

Foundational Pivot
1950 – 1963

Columbia University Professorship & Discrete Series

Served on the faculty of Columbia University. Here he established the characters of representations of semisimple Lie groups, the Plancherel formula for complex semisimple groups, and began the formulation of discrete series representations.

Columbia University
1963 – 1983

IBM von Neumann Professor at IAS Princeton & Honors

Appointed Permanent Member and subsequently IBM von Neumann Professor at the Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton. Elected Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1973, awarded the Cole Prize by the American Mathematical Society, and received the Ramanujan Medal. He passed away at Princeton on October 16, 1983.

IAS Princeton · FRS
TODAY

Enduring Foundation of 21st-Century Mathematics

Harish-Chandra's work forms the non-negotiable bedrock of the Langlands Program, modern quantum field theory, and automorphic forms. His birth city honors him through the Harish-Chandra Research Centre at CSJMU Kanpur.
